Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is a dimensionality reduction technique used to transform high-dimensional data into a lower-dimensional representation while retaining the most important variance in the dataset. By doing so, PCA helps in visualizing complex data, reducing computational cost, and eliminating noise. The main idea behind PCA is to identify new axes (principal components) that maximize the variance in the dataset, effectively summarizing the original features with fewer dimensions.

Clustering Analysis

Clustering is an unsupervised machine learning technique used to group data points based on their similarities. In this project, clustering helps identify **patterns in asteroid trajectories**, allowing for **early detection of potential collision risks**.

Types of Clustering Methods

We explored three clustering methods: K-Means, Hierarchical Clustering, and DBSCAN. Each method provides unique insights into asteroid movement patterns.

K-Means Clustering

  • Partition-based method: Divides asteroids into k clusters based on trajectory features like velocity and distance.
  • Distance metric: Uses Euclidean distance to assign points to clusters.
  • Strengths: Efficient for large datasets, useful for identifying asteroid groups with similar motion.
  • Weaknesses: Assumes clusters are spherical, struggles with irregular asteroid paths.

Hierarchical Clustering

  • Tree-based method: Builds a hierarchical representation of asteroid clusters.
  • Distance metric: Uses various metrics (Euclidean, Manhattan) to form clusters.
  • Strengths: No need to predefine the number of clusters, useful for detecting nested asteroid groups.
  • Weaknesses: Computationally expensive for large asteroid datasets.

DBSCAN (Density-Based Clustering)

  • Density-based method: Identifies asteroid clusters based on density.
  • Distance metric: Uses epsilon (ε) radius and minimum points (minPts) for clustering.
  • Strengths: Detects asteroid clusters of arbitrary shapes and is robust to noise (e.g., outliers or erratic asteroids).
  • Weaknesses: Requires careful tuning of ε and minPts to balance detection accuracy.

Clustering in Asteroid Collision Prediction

In our Asteroid Collision Prediction project, clustering is crucial for:

  • Grouping asteroids with similar trajectories.
  • Identifying high-risk asteroid clusters that may pose collision threats.
  • Analyzing patterns in historical asteroid paths to predict future behavior.

Association Rule Mining (ARM)

Association Rule Mining (ARM) is a data mining technique aimed at uncovering interesting relationships, patterns, and associations within large datasets. It is widely used in various domains, such as market basket analysis, to identify sets of items that frequently co-occur in transactions.​ ARM relies on three key metrics to evaluate the strength of association rules:

  • Support: The proportion of transactions in which a given rule holds true.
  • Confidence: The probability that the rule's consequent is true given that the antecedent is true.
  • Lift: The strength of an association rule relative to its expected probability.

Association Rules

An association rule is an implication of the form {A} → {B}, where A and B are disjoint itemsets. The rule suggests that the presence of itemset A in a transaction implies the presence of itemset B. The strength and significance of such rules are evaluated using the measures of support, confidence, and lift.

The Apriori Algorithm

The Apriori algorithm is a foundational method in ARM, designed to identify frequent itemsets in large datasets and subsequently generate association rules. It operates on the principle that all non-empty subsets of a frequent itemset must also be frequent. This property allows the algorithm to efficiently prune the search space by eliminating itemsets that do not meet the minimum support threshold.​

How the Apriori Algorithm works:

  • Identify Frequent Individual Items: The algorithm begins by scanning the dataset to determine the support of individual items and identifies those that meet or exceed the minimum support threshold.​
  • Generate Candidate Itemsets:Using the frequent individual items, the algorithm generates candidate itemsets of length two and calculates their support.​
  • Repeat Iteratively:Steps 2 and 3 are repeated, increasing the length of the itemsets by one in each iteration, until no more frequent itemsets are found.​
  • Generate Association Rules:From the frequent itemsets identified, the algorithm generates association rules and computes their confidence and lift to assess their significance.​

    Association Rule Mining (ARM) Results

    Understanding the Metrics

    • Support: Frequency of the rule occurring in the dataset.
    • Confidence: Probability that the consequent occurs given the antecedent.
    • Lift: Measure of how much more likely the consequent is given the antecedent, compared to random chance.
    • Leverage: Difference between observed and expected frequency of rule occurrence.
    • Conviction: Measure of rule reliability (higher values indicate stronger associations).
    • Jaccard: Measures the similarity between the antecedent and consequent sets.
    • Kulczynski: Measures the balance between confidence of A → B and B → A.
